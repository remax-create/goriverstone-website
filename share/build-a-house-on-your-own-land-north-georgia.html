Own Land in North Georgia? How Building on It Works

Own land in North Georgia and want to build? How it works with Riverstone Homes: walking the land, picking a plan, construction-to-permanent loans and the build.

SW
Sandra WatkinsNew Home Guide, Riverstone Homes

Maybe it is a few acres Granddad left you outside Adairsville. Maybe it is a lot you bought near Calhoun years ago "for someday." Either way, a lot of folks across North Georgia are sitting on land and wondering the same thing: how do I actually get a house on it? Good news. If you build a house on your own land in Georgia, the path is a lot simpler than it looks from the road, and you do not have to figure it out alone. Here is how it works with Riverstone Homes.

Your land or ours: why owning land is a head start

At Riverstone our motto is "Your land or ours," and we mean both halves of it. When you already own your land, you have skipped one of the biggest steps in building a new home. You know the view, you know the neighbors, and you are not competing with anyone for the perfect lot. We build on clients' land all over North Georgia, from Cartersville and Adairsville to Calhoun, Rome and Jasper, and we handle the planning, permits and construction from start to finish.

Step 1: Walk the land together

Every build starts with a walk. We look at where the house will sit, which way it should face, the slope, the driveway, and how water, power and septic or sewer will get there. Those details shape your plan and your budget, so it is the first thing we do. If you are still shopping for land, our post on what to check before you buy a lot walks through the homework, and our find land page is a good place to start looking.

Step 2: Pick a plan (or bring your own)

Next comes the fun part. You can choose one of our proven plans, like the Walton, the Azalea Park, the Waterbury Cottage or the Hemlock Falls, or bring a plan you already love. Then you make it yours with finishes: siding colors, the kitchen island, flooring, tile, lighting and all the details that make a house feel like home. I help every buyer through selections so nothing gets missed and nothing feels overwhelming.

Step 3: Financing, explained simply

Most people who build on their own land use a construction-to-permanent loan. In plain words, it is one loan that pays for the build, then turns into a regular mortgage once the house is finished. Experian explains that this type of loan can cover land, materials, labor, permits and other building costs, and then automatically converts to a traditional mortgage when construction is complete.

A few things buyers are always glad to hear:

  • One closing. NewHomeSource points out that a one-time close loan means one closing and one set of closing costs.
  • Your land can help. If you already own your land, many lenders can count some or all of that land equity toward the loan. Rules vary by lender and loan program.
  • Interest only while you build. Freddie Mac notes that during the construction phase you typically pay interest only, not principal.

Every lender is a little different, so talk with a lender who does construction loans regularly, get pre-approved early, and bring your plan and budget along. We are happy to point you to lenders our buyers have had good experiences with.

Step 4: We build, you watch it come to life

Once the plan, budget and financing are set, we pull permits and get to work. Our superintendent is on site watching the details, and you will always know who to call. You get updates as the foundation goes in, the walls go up and your finishes come together. Then comes the final walkthrough, the keys, and your first night in a home built on your own land.

Quick answers

Can Riverstone build on land I already own?

Yes. We build on clients' land across North Georgia, including Bartow, Gordon, Cherokee, Pickens and Cobb counties.

Do I have to use one of your plans?

No. You can pick one of ours or bring your own plan, and we will build it.

Does my land have to be paid off?

Not always. Many construction loans can work with land that still has a balance. Your lender will walk you through the options.
