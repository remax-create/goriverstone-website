First Winter in a New Home? 7 Things Your Builder Wants You to Check

Moving into a new build this winter? Here are 7 first-winter checks from a builder: pipes, filters, water heater, thermostat and normal drywall cracks.

Congratulations on the new house. Now comes the part nobody puts in the brochure: your first winter in it. A brand new home is tight, well insulated and full of fresh materials that are still settling in. A few simple habits in the first cold months will save you money and headaches. Here is what we tell every Riverstone Homes buyer in North Georgia.

1. Find your main water shutoff before you need it

Walk through the house with your builder or your new home guide and put your hand on the main water shutoff. Know where the shutoffs are under each sink and toilet too. If a pipe ever does freeze or leak, the next sixty seconds matter, and you do not want to be hunting for a valve in the dark.

2. Disconnect the hoses and drain the outdoor faucets

This is the easiest freeze protection there is. UGA Extension says to disconnect garden hoses from exterior faucets, then shut off the water to the faucet and drain the pipe. A hose left on can trap water right up against the house, and it only takes one hard freeze to cause a problem. Do it before the first cold snap, not after.

3. Protect pipes on the coldest nights

North Georgia does not get deep winters, but we get plenty of nights in the low twenties. UGA Extension recommends opening cabinet doors where water pipes run, like under kitchen and bathroom sinks and in laundry closets, so warm air can reach them. They also suggest leaving at least one faucet dripping during freezing temperatures. Pipes on exterior walls are the ones to watch.

4. Check your HVAC filter and listen to the system

Your heating system works harder in winter, and a clogged filter makes it work harder still. Check the filter every month through the cold season and swap it when it looks dirty, following the size and instructions on your unit. While you are there, listen. Your system should start, run and shut off without loud banging or odd smells. If it does not, call your HVAC company early, not on the coldest night of the year.

5. Pick a thermostat setting and stick with it

Constant big swings make the system work overtime. The U.S. Department of Energy's educational materials say that adjusting your thermostat by two degrees can lower heating bills by about four percent. Pick a comfortable setting, nudge it down a little when you are asleep or away, and let it be.

6. Look at your water heater setting

The Department of Energy says that for most homes, a water heater set to 120 degrees saves energy and helps avoid scalding. Check the dial or display on your unit. If you have little ones in the house, that extra safety margin is worth a minute of your time.

7. Expect a few small drywall cracks and nail pops

Here is the one that surprises new owners. As lumber dries and the house adjusts to heating season, you may see hairline cracks near corners, doors and windows, or a nail pop in a wall or ceiling. A builder warranty page from Van Metre Homes describes these as common and generally cosmetic, especially in the first year, and notes that keeping indoor humidity between 30 and 50 percent can help limit them. Keep a simple list with the date and a photo, and bring it up at your one-year check-in with your builder.

Quick FAQ

Do new construction homes need to be winterized?

Not the way an empty cabin does, but the outdoor faucet and freeze habits above still apply. New homes are well insulated, yet pipes on exterior walls and outdoor spigots are still your weak spots on a hard freeze.

Are small cracks in the drywall a sign something is wrong?

Usually not. Hairline cracks and nail pops are common in a new home's first year as materials settle. Wide, growing or uneven cracks are different, so show those to your builder.

What should my water heater be set to?

The Department of Energy says 120 degrees is right for most homes.
